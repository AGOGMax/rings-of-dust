EPILOGUE III

Late October. The first snow was on the high peaks.

It had come quietly, the way the first snow always did — not with announcement but with the simple fact of itself, a whitening at the edges of the range that you noticed when you happened to look. Sharon had noticed it that morning from the kitchen window. She had not said anything about it. Some things did not require saying.

Rachel knocked once.

Cole opened the door before the second knock. He had been in the kitchen, had heard the car on the road from some distance. Old instinct. He did not know the car. He did not know the knock. He opened the door and Rachel Vayes was standing on the porch in a dark coat with her hands at her sides, and the Collective droplet stencil was on the railing, and the first snow was on the high peaks, and none of this was a surprise in the way it might have been six months ago.

"I wanted to thank you," she said.

She came inside for thirty minutes. She sat in the chair by the window — the one Sharon had been sitting in when she'd said Hi — and drank a cup of coffee and was composed and warm and entirely herself. She thanked Cole for not dying when he had been supposed to. She thanked Sharon for coming back from something that should have been permanent. She did not explain how she had found the house, because she was not the kind of person who explained that. She asked about the arm. Sharon showed her the calibration. Rachel watched with the expression of someone who has looked at a great number of difficult things and learned not to look away.

It was a short visit.

She stood to go. She said what she had come to say and she was finished. She shook Cole's hand and she held Sharon's left hand for a moment — the one with the ring — and then she turned toward the door.

Before she moved, she set something on the counter beside the coffee cups — small, folded, dark background. A pair of sunglasses in white outline thread.

She didn't explain it. "For Sam," she said. "When you see her."

Cole looked at it.

"She earned it," Rachel said.

Her coat shifted.

The inside of the jacket was visible for just a moment — a half-turn, a breath of motion. The patch was there. Bronze star, olive leaf wreath. Both of them saw it. Cole's eyes moved to Sharon's. Sharon's moved back.

Nobody said anything about it.

She had never been hiding who she was. She had been hiding where she stood. She didn't betray Marcus. She was never his.

Rachel said goodbye. The door closed. Her footsteps on the porch, then the sound of the car, then nothing.

The kitchen was quiet. Outside, the first snow was still on the high peaks. The Collective droplet stencil was on the porch railing. It had been there when they moved in and neither of them had asked about it and they were not going to ask about it now.

Sharon turned from the window.

"It was always in the reaching," she said.

She reached.
